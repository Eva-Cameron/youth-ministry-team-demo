    What is Adoration?

    What happens in Adoration at The Source, and why Catholics spend time there.

    Part of every night at The Source is Adoration. If you have never been, this is what is going on.

    Jesus, really present

    At Mass, Catholics believe the bread and wine become the Body and Blood of Jesus. He is really there, not just remembered. The consecrated bread is called the Host, or the Blessed Sacrament.

    In Adoration the Host is placed in a monstrance on the altar. A monstrance is a stand, often gold, with a small window in the middle so everyone can see the Host. Then we simply spend time with Jesus.

    What do you actually do?

    There is no right way. You can sit, kneel or stand. You can pray in your own words, say a prayer you know, read a short passage from the Bible, listen to the music or just be quiet. Some people bring a notebook and write.

    If your mind wanders, that is normal. Notice it and come back. Even a few minutes of real quiet is a gift.

    Why do it?

    Most of life is busy and loud. Adoration is time when you do not have to perform or prove anything. Many people say it is where they first felt that God knows them and loves them as they are.

    At the end there is often Benediction: the priest blesses everyone with the Host before it is put away.

    Words to know

    Host
    The consecrated bread from Mass.
    Blessed Sacrament
    Another name for the Host.
    Monstrance
    The stand that holds the Host so people can see it.
    Genuflect
    Kneeling briefly on one knee as a sign of respect.
    Benediction
    The blessing with the Host at the end of Adoration.
